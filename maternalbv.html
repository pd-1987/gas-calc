Clinical Pearl: Uterine contraction immediately post-delivery results in an autotransfusion of ~500 mL into the systemic circulation. Combined with maternal hypervolaemia, this allows women to compensate for massive blood loss, often masking the signs of shock until a critical cliff-edge is reached.

Transfusion Targets (RCOG / OAA)
Haemoglobin > 80 g/L
Fibrinogen* > 2.0 g/L
Platelets > 75 × 109/L
PT / APTT < 1.5× normal

*Fibrinogen: Pregnancy is a prothrombotic state with a high baseline fibrinogen (4–6 g/L). It drops rapidly during major haemorrhage, so the obstetric target (> 2.0 g/L) is higher than the standard adult target.

Note: In ongoing major obstetric haemorrhage, empiric blood products (e.g., Major Haemorrhage Packs) should be administered immediately without waiting for laboratory results.

Major Haemorrhage Packs (MHP)
Pack A (Pack 1) 4 PRBC + 4 FFP
(+ 1g IV TXA*)
Pack B (Pack 2) 4 PRBC + 4 FFP
1 Platelets + 2 Cryoprecipitate**

*TXA Redosing: If bleeding continues after 30 min, or stops and restarts within 24 h of the first dose, a second dose of 1 g of tranexamic acid can be given.

**Cryoprecipitate / Fibrinogen Concentrate: Request early with Pack A if FibTem / lab indicates rapid fibrinogen depletion (< 2.0 g/L).
